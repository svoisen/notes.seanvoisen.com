There's an important distinction between *place* and *space* in the context design context: space is purely physical, but place includes the social dimensions and aspects of a space. Place "reflects the emergence of presence" in a space. That is, **the knowledge that imbues a sense of place emerges as part of collective behavior over time**.

Online or virtual collaborative environments are often envisioned as a kind of space, but they are not physical. This metaphor primarily acts as a shortcut for the development of shared meaning in the space, hopefully imbuing it with a sense of place as people use it for collective purpose.

There are frameworks, such as Geraldine Fitzpatrick's [Locales Framework](https://pages.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/~saul/581/exer.heuristic_groupware/Locales%20Framework.pdf), that help designers take into account the social organization of an activity when designing spaces.
