[[Offices and workspaces are complex ecologies of work]], the arrangement of which often reveal the nature of the people who work within them. David Kirsh places physical office arrangements on a spectrum between two polar opposites he calls "neat" and "scruffy" (based on the same terms used in AI research).

One significant differentiator between neat and scruffy offices is the number, arrangement and visibility of "entry points"—physical invitations or cues to engage in specific tasks. Neats tend to keep offices tidy, filing things away, and keeping desks cleaned up and orderly at the end of the day. Scruffies, on the other hand, tend to leave piles of papers, file folders, and post-its strewn about. As a result, the number of visible "entry points"—that is, affordances which signal or invite work on a particular task—vary greatly. Neat offices tend to have few visible entry points, but scruffy offices have many.

There are different costs and benefits in each:

* Neat offices offer entry points at standard, canonical locations like filing cabinets, journals, organizers, inboxes, etc.
* Scruffy offices have more entry points, and more opportunity for serendipitous encounters with information
* Scruffies, however, pay a cost in search time for information while rummaging through papers. This may also have some negative consequences for metacognition, because a messy environment may pull someone out of flow as they are forced to stop and constant reassess or monitor what they are doing due to distraction (e.g. having to move things around, get distracted by things in the periphery, etc.) [[Good design reduces metacognitive overhead]] and a "scruffy" space is generally not "well-designed" for any specific task. (This is why good chefs have a mise en place.)

It may be worth analyzing the parallels between offices and digital tools as enabling environments, and whether the costs and benefits have similar parallels.

For instance, I have a hunch that [[Digital collaborative environments tend to be neat]], and as such [[The opportunity for serendipitous encounters in many digital information spaces is low]].
