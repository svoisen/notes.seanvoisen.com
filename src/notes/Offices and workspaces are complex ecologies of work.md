The physical layout of an office is the context of the work performed within it. A well-used workspace has deep structure—a spatial arrangement that reveals not only the type of work that takes place within, but also how that work is performed and insight into the nature of the people performing the work. According to Kirsh, workspaces display structural coupling (drawing from Maturana and his work on enactivism), meaning that they exhibit (visually) a relationship between their physical environment and the cognitive work that goes into them. What's more, this structural coupling changes and shifts over time as the activities in an office change.

Can this be translated into and used in the design of virtual environments or "virtual offices?" Kirsh asserts that in order to do so, we need to look for the components of the office structure that can be translated into "psychologically accessible" structure. He enumerates four such things:

- Entry points (See also [[There are more activity entry points in messy (scruffy) spaces]])
- Coordinative structures
- The shape of activity spaces
- The actual information resources

On the topic of activity entry points, specifically, [[Digital collaborative environments tend to be neat]] rather than messy, meaning that [[The opportunity for serendipitous encounters in many digital information spaces is low]]. Because of this, I often feel that shared digital spaces tend not to exhibit the same visible structures of a "complex ecology" as well as physical spaces. Information that would otherwise be ambient and observable is tucked away or hidden rather than encoded in "spatial arrangements."

But Kirsh seems to say we have to go beyond what we see in any analysis anyway. We have to go beyond the "simple facts of who or what is where and when" because some of the context of the complex ecology may not be directly observable in the physical environment. For instance, resources could be purely conceptual or shared knowledge between two inhabitants of the workspace. Design of virtual workspaces will have to take this into account.
