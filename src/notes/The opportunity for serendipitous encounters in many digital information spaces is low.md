[[Digital collaborative environments tend to be neat]] and as such the number of activity entry points they visibly expose is low. This is because [[There are more activity entry points in messy (scruffy) spaces]].

This creates a problem for the serendipitous encounter of information, which often sparks new ideas or creative connections. There's a reason that many workshops and design studios have plenty of "stuff" out and visible on shelves, benches, and tables—it primes the imagination and promotes creative thinking through "perceptual rehearsal."

[[Physical space can act as scaffolding for creative work]], and there are parallels between the design of physical and virtual space to facilitate creativity. If this is true, then a "messy" virtual space is likely to perform better at stimulating creativity than a "neat" space.

One of the advantages of digital spaces, however, is that you can have both neat and scruffy "views" on the same information! You can effectively toggle between neat and scruffy at will. There are a few examples of this in the wild, Obsidian's canvas being but one. A canvas can be messy; the user can splay out a variety of cards representing notes in an infinite 2D space, moving them about and grouping them at will, taking advantage of human spatial memory and reasoning to make sense of large amounts of information. At the same time, an Obsidian user can also make lists and tables of the same data.

Unfortunately, most digital tools fail to support this kind of multi-view (scruffy and neat) interface. They tend to impose neatness ([[Digital collaborative environments tend to be neat]]) and eschew messiness entirely. But if [[The GUI was invented as a tool for thought]] then this is truly a failure of design. How can we better support both neat and scruffy digital environments in the same tool while ensuring that users don't accidentally bury themselves in an avalanche of mess? (For instance, the typical computer desktop buried under thousands of scattered icons.)
